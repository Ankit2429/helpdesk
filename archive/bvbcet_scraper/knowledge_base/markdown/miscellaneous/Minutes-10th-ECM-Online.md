To discuss the status report of constituent colleges 
(Information agenda). 
KLE Technological University has completed 5 years of operation and is eligible to 
establish Constituent College, additional campuses, Regional Centres, or Study Centres 
as per the provisions in para 6 of the KLE Technological University Act 2012 (Karnataka 
Act No 22 of 2013). The extract of the Rajyapatra is given below. 
  "6. Power to establish constituent College, additional campuses, Regional Centres or 
Study Centres.- The University may have Constituent Colleges, Regional Centres, 
additional campuses, and Study Centres at such places in the State as it deems fit 
after the completion of five years after its establishment with prior approval of the 
State Government subject to norms of UGC and other National Accreditation bodies". 
  With the permission of the BOG, KLE Technological University applied for establishing 
constituent colleges at Belagavi and Bengaluru by making KLE Dr. M.S. Sheshgiri College 
of Engineering and Technology, Belagavi and K.L.E Society's Law College, Bengaluru as 
the constituent colleges/units of KLE Technological University, Hubballi. The 
Government of Karnataka has accorded the permission to establish constituent 
colleges at Belagavi and Bengaluru from the academic year 2021-21 for the current 
intake of both UG & PG programs. 
  The status report of the constituent colleges will be presented during the meeting. 
  Action Requested: The Executive Council is requested to note the status report of the 
constituent colleges. 
  Discussion: Prof. Ashok Shettar, the Vice-chancellor, presented the status report of the 
constituent colleges. Further, he said there are two ways to bring other colleges into the KLE 
Technological University. One way is as a constituent college, and another way is as an additional 
campus of the University. 
Dr. Madhu Atre asked whether the regulations and the syllabi of the constituent are the same or 
different. In reply, the Vice-chancellor said they would be the same. 
To a question on benchmarking of the law college, the Vice-chancellor said we are currently looking 
at O. P . Jindal Global University Sonipat, Haryana. 
  Resolution 10.3: The EC noted the status report of the constituent 
colleges.

  Action Requested: The Executive Council is requested to discuss the research progress

  Discussion: Prof. Uma M. Dean (R&D) presented the progress of R&D activities during 2020 
and 2021. 
Referring to the evolving research guide (ERG), Prof. Atre asked whether a process is in place to 
guide the new ERG's by allotting only one candidate. Further, he emphasized mentoring to make an 
ERG a good guide. In reply, Dean (R&D) mentioned that the research progress review by the external 
mentors is in place, and the number of internal Senior Research Guides (SRG) is less to guide the 
ERGs. Prof. Ashok Shettar, the Vice-chancellor, expressed that the external experts barring a few are 
good reviewers but not able to guide and handhold the ERGs. 
Prof. Madhu Atre opined that supporting IREF is very important & critical. He asked Dean (R&D) to 
take this as an action point to discuss in the next 'Research Council' meeting. 
Prof. Atre, referring to the IREF model of KLE Tech., enquired whether the three-year preparatory 
period before the formal registration for P .hD. is not too long. The Vice-chancellor mentioned that 
for well-performing IREF's after one or two years, we have been permitting to register for the P .hD. 
programme. 
Prof. Madhu Atre asked Dean (R&D) to take a second action point regarding preparing a half or one-
page document containing the expectations from external experts in 5 to 6 bullets with 
descriptions, time, and type of engagement. His suggestion was, focus on ERGs who in turn will 
influence IREFs. 
Referring to the number of patents filed and granted, Prof. Atre asked what has come out of these 
patents. In reply, the Vice-chancellor said so far, the focus was on NIRF, and we have not yet looked 
into the monetization process or technology transfer or leading to collaboration. Prof. Atre 
suggested raising this issue while discussing it with Prof Ravi Guttal, Director, CIPD.

Credit system 
  20 Semester-wise credit-based system shall be followed in 
each programme of study except in the case of very-short 
non-degree programmes. 
  20. 1 Generally, credits shall be assigned to the each course in a 
programme of study based on the following pattern: 
a) Lectures: One lecture hour per week shall be assigned one credit b) Tutorials: Two tutorial hours per week shall be assigned one credit c) Practicals: Two laboratory hours per week shall be assigned one credit. Not more than three credits may be assigned to a practical course having only laboratory component. The courses having three hours of contact every alternate week shall have one credit only d) Mini-projects, Minor-projects, Seminars, Projects: Three contact and preparation hours per week shall be assigned one credit e) Dissertation : Three contact and preparation hours 
 ## Page 33

  per week shall be assigned one credit f) Legal Aid Work: There can be a self-study component in the curriculum as decided by the DUGC/DPGC. g) Self Study: There can be a self-study component in the curriculum as decided by the DUGC/DPGC. 
  20. 2 The course credits for each course shall be given as L-T-P . 
For example, 3-1-0 will mean that it is a lecture based 
course and has 3 lectures, 1 tutorial, and no practical 
assigned to it. Similarly, a course with 0-0-2 means that it 
is a practical course with 4 hours of class work. Credits will 
be assigned to seminar, dissertation, project etc. under 
the practical component. 
Example: Course 21LBLC101 Constitutional Law; 5 credits 
(4-1-0). The hours for this course are computed as follows: 
4 credits = 4 hours/week lectures 
1 credit = 2 hours/week tutorial 
0 credits = 0 × 2 = 0 hours/week practicals 
Total = 4 + 2 + 0 = 6 hours / week 
Total contact hours for the course = (4 h Lectures + 2 h 
Tutorial + 0 h Practical) per week = 6 contact hours per 
week for 16 weeks.

SGPA and CGPA 
  65 The overall performance of a student shall be indicated by 
two indices namely, Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) 
and Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA). 
  65. 1 The SGPA is the weighted average of the grade points 
obtained in all courses registered by the student during a 
particular semester . The SGPA shall be calculated as: SGPA 
= Σ CiGi / Σ Ci, where Ci‟s, are the number of credits for 
the courses registered for the semester, and Gi‟s are the 
corresponding grade points secured by the student. 
  65. 2 The CGPA is an indication of an up-to-date overall 
performance of a student and the weighted average of 
the grade points obtained in all the courses registered by 
the student since he/she is admitted to the University. It 
shall be calculated as: CGPA = Σ CiGi / Σ Ci, the summation 
is carried out for all courses to which the student has 
registered up to that point of time, excluding courses with 
"F" & "FA" grades and transitional grades. 
  65. 3 If a student obtains an "F" grade in a course and registers 
for the same course in a subsequent semester, the new 
grade obtained shall replace the previous "F" grade in 
calculating the CGPA. 
  65. 4 If a student obtains an "F" grade in an elective course and 
registers for the same or equivalent course in a 
subsequent semester, the new grade obtained shall 
replace the previous "F" grade in calculating the CGPA.
